Reading This Chart

Normal traffic (76.49%) outnumbers the rarest class, road_block (0.53%), by roughly 145×. This is the exact imbalance the class-weighting and rarity-bonus terms in Figure 5 exist to counteract — worth referencing this figure directly when introducing those terms in Section III-D.

Figure 9 · Federated Training Convergence

Macro F1 Across 30 Communication Rounds

Note on What "Variance" Means Here

The two curves are nearly indistinguishable from round ~15 onward — this is real, plotted directly from your CSVs. Section V-B's "Macro F1 variance of 0.0011 vs 0.0010" describes round-to-round variance within this single run, not variance across independent seeds. No seed files exist in the current results directory — add 2-3 more seeded runs before claiming statistical robustness in the paper.

Figure 10 · Confusion Matrix — AdaptiveFedAvg

8-Class Classification, Held-Out Test Set

What the Off-Diagonal Cells Say

road_damage → normal (29) and normal → road_damage (160) are the largest confusions, and normal → diversion (237) stands out sharply — worth a sentence in Section V explaining why diversion specifically gets misread as normal at this rate, since it's currently unexplained. road_block (16/24 correct) and flood (45/47 correct) perform far better than their tiny sample counts would suggest is statistically reliable — flag this the same way Section IV-A's rare-class warning already does.
